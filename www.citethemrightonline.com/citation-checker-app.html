      Artificial intelligence (AI) continues to transform how society functions across sectors such as education, healthcare, and media (Smith, 2020). Recent studies suggest that adaptive learning platforms can personalise student instruction, leading to improved academic outcomes (Johnson and Clark, 2019). In healthcare, machine learning models have demonstrated high accuracy in diagnostic imaging (Lee, Kim and Park, 2018). However, Brown et al. (2021) warn that bias in AI systems can reproduce existing inequalities if not carefully managed. According to the Department for Education (2021), AI should be used to complement, not replace, teachers. Taylor (2020) highlights the ethical dilemmas surrounding data collection, while Anderson, Hall and Moore (2022) emphasise the importance of transparency and explainability in algorithmic decision-making. Further research is needed to explore long-term social consequences (Brown et al., 2021). Finally, Smith (2020) notes that effective AI policy requires collaboration between governments and researchers to ensure equitable outcomes.
